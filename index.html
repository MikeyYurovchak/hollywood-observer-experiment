THE PASTA THAT ALMOST ENDED A CAREER!

Hollywood has seen its fair share of scandals, explosions, and questionable haircuts, but nothing could have prepared the world for the afternoon Professor EJ Néel nearly sent legendary actor John Cusack to the great movie premiere in the sky!

In this entirely fictional chain of events, an innocent afternoon allegedly spiraled into a cinematic catastrophe involving a rogue shopping cart, 47 pounds of spaghetti, a malfunctioning fog machine, and one extremely confused Hollywood superstar.

And it all started with a simple misunderstanding.

According to a completely imaginary eyewitness who claims to have been hiding inside a vending machine, Cusack had arrived on campus to discuss the finer points of acting when Professor Néel made what may go down in history as the worst decision since someone invented the self-checkout machine.

“THAT'S NOT HOW PHYSICS WORKS!”

The professor was reportedly demonstrating the laws of motion by launching a shopping cart down a hallway. Unfortunately, the cart had experimental roller-skate wheels and was carrying 47 pounds of slippery pasta.

Its destination? A table directly in front of John Cusack.
Its speed? Approximately 900 miles per hour, according to a calculator that was immediately confiscated.
Its brakes? One rubber band and a prayer.

CUSACK: ONE MEATBALL FROM DISASTER!

Witnesses described the scene as “like Speed, except with more carbohydrates and significantly less budget.” Cusack, reportedly holding a coffee and contemplating independent cinema, looked up just in time to see the oncoming pasta-powered death machine.

“Is this part of the movie?” he allegedly asked.

IT WAS NOT.

In a desperate attempt to stop the cart, Professor Néel grabbed a nearby fire extinguisher. Unfortunately, it was not a fire extinguisher. It was a leaf blower someone had painted red.

A hurricane of spaghetti blasted through the hallway. Meatballs ricocheted off lockers. Parmesan filled the air like a blizzard. One undergraduate was reportedly propelled backward into a recycling bin, where he remained for eleven minutes before emerging with a newfound appreciation for organic chemistry.

And John Cusack? He was launched backward onto a conveniently placed inflatable dinosaur, which bounced him through double doors and into a pile of beanbags.

The actor was alive. His coffee was not.

“I'VE BEEN IN WEIRD MOVIES, BUT THIS IS RIDICULOUS!”

After narrowly surviving the incident, Cusack reportedly stood up, brushed three pounds of linguine off his jacket, and stared at Professor Néel in disbelief.

“I've been in 1408. I've been in Con Air. I've even been in Being John Malkovich,” the fictional actor reportedly declared. “But I've never been attacked by a carbohydrate before!”

The professor allegedly attempted to explain the situation using a whiteboard, three equations, and a drawing of a potato.

“It was a controlled experiment!” Néel reportedly insisted.

“THEN WHY IS THERE A MEATBALL IN MY SHOE?” Cusack allegedly replied.

An emergency response team arrived, consisting of two theater majors, a confused cafeteria worker, and a campus tour guide who had mistaken the incident for an extracurricular activity. They attempted CPR on Cusack's ruined coffee.

It did not survive.

THE COVER-UP: PROJECT AL DENTE?

Just when investigators believed the scandal couldn't become more ridiculous, they allegedly discovered a folder in the professor's office labeled PROJECT AL DENTE.

Inside were 37 pages of handwritten equations, a picture of a shopping cart wearing sunglasses, and a sticky note reading: “IF CUSACK ASKS QUESTIONS, DISTRACT HIM WITH GARLIC BREAD.”

Coincidence? We think NOT!

Was this an innocent scientific demonstration gone wrong? An elaborate film stunt? Or was Professor Néel secretly attempting to create the world's first pasta-powered transportation system?

When asked for comment, the professor allegedly responded, “I can explain everything.” She then tripped over the leaf blower.

Investigators are also examining reports that the shopping cart may have been operating under the influence of extra-virgin olive oil.

HOLLYWOOD'S STRANGEST SURVIVOR SPEAKS!

In the days following the imaginary incident, Cusack reportedly vowed never to visit a university again unless the cafeteria agreed to serve meals in bowls made of reinforced steel.

“I thought the biggest threat to my life would be a bad movie review,” he allegedly said. “I never imagined I'd be taken out by Italian cuisine.”

Meanwhile, Professor Néel has supposedly been banned from operating shopping carts, leaf blowers, and any object with wheels until further notice.

PASTA LA VISTA, BABY!

The cafeteria has introduced a new menu item in honor of the imaginary incident: The Cusack Calamity Combo, featuring spaghetti, meatballs, garlic bread, and a complimentary liability waiver.

FINAL VERDICT: ONE MEATBALL FROM HISTORY!

As this fictional scandal continues to rock the imaginary entertainment world, one question remains unanswered: How close did John Cusack come to becoming the first Hollywood star ever defeated by a bowl of spaghetti?

Experts say the answer is simple: one meatball, two roller skates, and approximately three seconds of poor decision-making.

Cusack has allegedly installed a personal pasta-detection system in his home. And somewhere in a university hallway, a shopping cart is still rolling.
